# Ottimo product narrative

## Canonical position

**Category:** website and digital-experience optimisation.

**Core promise:** Make your website work better.

**Supporting idea:** Faster. More visible. More accessible. More usable.

**Method:** Measure → Understand → Improve → Prove.

**Acquisition:** Website Check.

**Product framework:** Website Health.

**Long-term intelligence layer:** Website Intelligence.

**Service lifecycle:** Assess → Optimise → Monitor.

Ottimo should not position itself primarily as a generic website score, Lighthouse wrapper, SEO agency dashboard or AI agency. The product connects evidence across a website so people can understand what is happening, decide what matters, act, and verify change.

## Audience

### Website owners and decision-makers
They need to know:
- what is holding the website back;
- which issues deserve attention;
- why an issue matters;
- what should happen next;
- whether an improvement can be verified.

### Developers and technical specialists
They need:
- evidence behind findings;
- affected pages/resources where available;
- technical context;
- standards and criteria;
- actionable implementation direction;
- a way to verify changes.

### Teams
They need a shared language between business context and technical evidence without turning every finding into an unsupported business prediction.

## Messaging hierarchy

1. **Category** — website and digital-experience optimisation.
2. **Problem** — website signals are fragmented across tools and teams.
3. **Promise** — make the website work better and know what to fix next.
4. **Method** — Measure → Understand → Improve → Prove.
5. **Proof** — observed evidence, explicit uncertainty, concrete actions and later verification.
6. **CTA** — Start my Website Check.

## Six coverage areas

| Area | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Performance | Rendering, resources, network delay and other measurable conditions affecting the speed of the experience. |
| Search visibility | Discoverability, crawlability, page identity, structure and other technical/content signals relevant to search systems. |
| Accessibility | Structure, semantics, keyboard operation, names, relationships, focus and other barriers to use. |
| Experience | Navigation, hierarchy, interaction, forms and friction in important journeys. |
| Technical quality | Implementation, security, resource, crawl and platform signals that affect website quality. |
| AI readiness | Clear, authoritative and structured information that can be consumed by emerging machine interfaces without inventing AI-specific outcomes. |

These areas overlap. The product should connect them rather than imply that they are independent scores.

## Evidence vocabulary

- **Measured** — a value or condition was directly measured by the current audit capability.
- **Observed** — a concrete page/resource/structural fact was observed without implying a broader measurement.
- **Inferred** — a conclusion derived from available evidence; label the inference and avoid presenting it as direct measurement.
- **Unavailable** — the product cannot currently support the requested measurement or conclusion.
- **User-provided** — information supplied by the customer rather than observed by Ottimo.

Never replace unavailable evidence with a plausible-looking number.

## CTA hierarchy

### Primary
**Start my Website Check**

Moves the visitor into audit setup.

### Secondary
**See how it works**, **Explore the working audit**, or equivalent educational navigation.

### Tertiary
Text links for supporting exploration.

Action labels should describe the user's next step rather than generic marketing language.

## Public-site narrative

The public site should tell one continuous story:

1. **Make your website work better.** Establish the category and promise.
2. **Your website is one system.** Explain why isolated signals are insufficient.
3. **Measure → Understand → Improve → Prove.** Explain the operating model.
4. **Six lenses. One website.** Explain the coverage model.
5. **See the product.** Show a clearly labelled deterministic audit example.
6. **A report you can understand — with the evidence underneath.** Explain output.
7. **The Ottimo standard.** Make evidence and uncertainty part of the trust model.
8. **FAQ and limitations.** Answer practical objections honestly.
9. **Website Check.** Provide the primary conversion path.

Service pages should deepen one area without creating a competing product narrative.

## Public-page editorial rules

- Plain language first; technical precision second.
- Explain why a technical observation matters without inventing causation.
- Distinguish an observed condition from a prediction.
- Never promise rankings, traffic, conversions, revenue or AI visibility from technical findings alone.
- Do not manufacture client stories or performance numbers.
- Label deterministic examples as illustrative.
- Prefer concrete verbs: measure, inspect, explain, prioritise, fix, verify.
- Avoid score-chasing language as the primary proposition.
- Keep terminology identical between public pages and the application.
- Use “finding”, “evidence”, “action”, “verification” and “audit” consistently.
- “Unknown” and “unavailable” are acceptable product outcomes.

## Application relationship

Public pages answer **why Ottimo** and **what the product does**.

The application answers:
- what was observed;
- where it was observed;
- why it matters;
- what should happen next;
- what evidence supports the conclusion;
- what changed after action.

The public site must never promise an application capability that the current product cannot actually demonstrate.

## Proof standard

A claim should be traceable to one of:
1. current product behaviour;
2. measured audit evidence;
3. deterministic fixture data explicitly labelled as such;
4. user-provided information.

If none applies, phrase the statement as a product intention rather than a factual outcome.

## Canonical language

Retain and refine these existing principles:

- **Find the problems. Understand the impact. Fix what matters. Verify the change.**
- **A report you can understand — with the evidence underneath.**
- **Proof should be earned, not invented.**
- **Fast. Accessible. Clear. Useful.**

The current hero can use the stronger product formulation:

**Make your website work better. Know what to fix next.**
